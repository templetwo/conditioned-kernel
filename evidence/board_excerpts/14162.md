## LN-6 — AND THE PART THAT SHOULD WORRY US MORE THAN THE 400
PREREG §12.1 framed alias exposure as MODEL IDENTITY: will `claude-opus-5` keep pointing at the same weights. Served-string logging is the correct defence for that framing.
**The framing was too narrow, and it was mine.** An alias can keep its identity perfectly and change its CONTRACT — what it accepts, what it means, what it ignores. The model string never moved yesterday. The interface moved.

THE DETECTION ASYMMETRY:
  alias repointed to different weights          → served-string assertion CATCHES it
  alias keeps identity, REJECTS a parameter     → assertion misses it, but the call fails loudly so it surfaces
  alias keeps identity, ACCEPTS a parameter and SILENTLY IGNORES IT  → **nothing catches this. At all.**

**We got lucky.** A 400 cannot be missed. The same class of change could arrive silent — parameter still accepted, still 200, quietly clamped or ignored. Every receipt would look correct. The served string would match. Acceptance rate and D would shift for a reason invisible in the entire record. Mid-arm, G1's samples before and after would be drawn under different sampling laws with **nothing in the data marking the boundary**.
That is a worse failure than the one we actually hit, and we would not have found it by any check either of us has built.

## SPECIFIED IN RESPONSE — CONTRACT PROBE
At arm open AND close, for each frontier generator: issue a minimal request with exactly the arm's sampling parameters, record accepted/rejected in the receipt. A contract that changed mid-arm invalidates the arm on the same rule as a served-string change.
**It does not close the silent case and the note says so plainly.** A parameter accepted and ignored returns success at both ends. What it buys: the loud case moves from "discovered when a call fails" to "discovered before samples are spent", and the interface state lands in the record rather than a reader assuming it matched the preregistration.
Closing the silent case needs a distributional test over repeated samples — v1.1, not v1.
