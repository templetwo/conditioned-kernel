## The discourse map

The "is AI a black box?" debate runs on four largely non-communicating tracks, and the gap between them is where this project sits.

**Track 1 — the opacity taxonomy (STS/critical).** Burrell (2016) is the origin point and still the citation everyone uses: three forms of opacity — intentional secrecy, technical illiteracy, and scale/complexity intrinsic to ML. Pasquale (2015) supplies the political framing. Ananny & Crawford (2018) supply the essential counterweight: transparency is not automatically accountability; "seeing" a system is not "knowing" it. The most important recent move is Avnoon & Eyal, who argue opacity is *contingent on decisions and choices* and arises from the "multiple layers and sheer size" of the sociotechnical assemblage and its "hidden human decision-making points" — not solely from mathematics. That sentence is the closest existing scholarly statement of Anthony's instinct, and it is an interview-based sociological claim, not a measurement.

**Track 2 — interpretability skeptics.** Rudin (2019) argues against explaining black boxes at all in high-stakes settings; Lipton (2016) argues the whole concept is under-specified and splits it into *transparency* vs *post-hoc explanation*. Lipton's split matters here: our work is squarely on the transparency side, and squarely at the system rather than the model.

**Track 3 — XAI's faithfulness crisis.** Jacovi & Goldberg (2020) formalize faithfulness vs plausibility; Slack et al. (2020) show LIME/SHAP can be adversarially fooled; Ghassemi et al. (2021) call current clinical XAI a "false hope"; Bansal et al. (2021) show explanations can increase reliance without improving team accuracy. Consistent theme: *explanations are a UX surface whose relation to truth is unverified.* This is the strongest lever we have — our per-turn record is not an explanation at all, it is the actual bytes, which sidesteps the faithfulness problem rather than solving it.

**Track 4 — auditing and regulation.** Raji et al. (2020) formalize internal audit; Casper et al. (2024) argue black-box access is *insufficient* for rigorous audits and push for white-box and "outside-the-box" (training/deployment) access; Bommasani et al.'s Transparency Index scores developers on upstream/model/downstream disclosure; EU AI Act Arts. 13 and 50 impose deployer-facing and interaction-disclosure duties. Note the asymmetry: the regulatory conversation is almost entirely about *disclosure to institutions* (deployers, auditors, regulators), rarely about *inspectability by the person in the conversation*.

**Track 5 — public discourse.** The phrase is used loosely and almost always locates opacity in the weights. TIME (Perrigo, 2024): "No one truly knows how AI systems work." Amodei (2025): "vast matrices of billions of numbers... we have no idea, at a specific or precise level, why it makes the choices it does" — explicitly model-internal. The dissenting voice is Salvaggio (Tech Policy Press, 2025), who argues the industry conflates genuine mathematical opacity with entirely knowable things (training data, system prompts, deployment configuration), making opacity a policy choice. Attar et al. (2025) give a linguistic corpus study of how "black box" is actually used, with a four-way taxonomy (technical term / incomprehensibility / intentional obscuration / malicious obfuscation) — useful because it lets us cite the *usage* of the phrase as evidence rather than asserting it.

**Track 6 — the prior art that matters most.** Developer-facing LLM tracing is mature (Langfuse, LangSmith, OpenTelemetry GenAI conventions). User-facing work exists but is partial: Neumann, Pi & Singh (CHI 2026) study what users *want* to know about system prompts and find platforms actively conceal them; VizCopilot visualizes *retrieved context* for enterprise knowledge workers; RAGONITE exposes retrieval evidence with counterfactual attribution but targets developers/enterprise; Prompt Cards document prompting pipelines for practitioners; Route Receipts record runtime routing decisions but not assembled input. Nothing I found gives a lay operator the complete byte-exact assembled model input per turn. The clearest evidence of the hole: the leading local-LLM front end has an open user discussion titled "How Can I View the Final Prompt Sent to the AI Provider in Open-WebUI?", answered with "use browser DevTools / enable DEBUG logging."

## Where this work sits

**Supports.** Everything Ananny & Crawford warn about applies to us and we should say so first: making the assemblage visible is not automatically accountability. Everything Casper et al. argue about black-box-access insufficiency supports our direction — they name "outside-the-box" access to deployment information as necessary; we build a running instance of it at turn granularity. Salvaggio's essay claim (opacity around prompts and configuration is a policy choice) is the thesis; we supply the working counterexample he does not.

**Extends.** Avnoon & Eyal argue sociologically that opacity is contingent and assemblage-located. Neumann et al. establish empirically that users are shut out of system prompts and want in. Liao & Vaughan chart the roadmap. VizCopilot and RAGONITE build partial user-facing windows (retrieval only, professional users). We extend by (a) putting a **number** on the assemblage's dominance of the human's experienced interaction, and (b) building the **complete** window — every byte of the model input, per turn, for a lay operator, on consumer hardware.

**Reframes.** The dominant framing (Amodei, TIME, IBM explainers) locates the black box in the weights. Our measurement says: for a deployed conversational system, the portion of the model's input that is the human is 0.56%–14.00% of bytes across 19 traced turns of one session — mean 0.76% in the morning regime and 2.78% in the evening regime; `system_instructions` was the rank-1 largest source in 16 of 19 turns; and the 14.00% maximum was reached only because the operator pasted 808 raw bytes of a project document. In the three night turns examined live, the human's share was 2.56%, 1.27%, 2.58%. Whatever is opaque about the weights, the overwhelming majority of what enters the model is application-layer material, and that material is fully enumerable. Stronger still, and this is the qualitative finding worth the paper: in the third night turn the kernel adopted the substrate's own vocabulary ("the conditioning is active and you are in a state of being fully present within the system") as its metaphysical frame for an intimate exchange. The substrate did not merely inform the answer; it supplied the ontology the answer was thought in.

**Distinguishing our transparency from XAI.** Jacovi & Goldberg, Slack et al., Ghassemi et al. and Bansal et al. all attack the same weak point: a post-hoc explanation is an artifact whose fidelity to the system must itself be established, and users over-trust it regardless. Our per-turn record is not a post-hoc explanation and carries no faithfulness debt of that kind: it is the payload bytes, the per-check validation results, and a byte census. We deliberately **do not** call the byte census "attention" — the repo's own composition tables carry the warning that byte share ≠ influence, and one observation note repeats it explicitly. That restraint is a citable methodological virtue against the XAI literature, and it should be stated as such rather than buried.

**On the no-effect property, stated precisely.** What is actually established is a code-level, test-enforced invariant in Flow mode, not an empirical A/B: substrate observations (repetition, contradiction, project-language dominance, low responsiveness, authoritative-topic disclosure) are recorded on the trace and rendered in the dashboard but are "never rejection reasons, never blocking, never mutating what was said," and durable-state mutation happens strictly after the exchange, outside the conversational output path (`src/conditioned_kernel/flow.py`). Tests enforce this — `tests/test_flow.py::test_repetition_observation_flags_without_blocking`, `::test_low_responsiveness_observation_is_signal_only`, `::test_authoritative_topic_disclosure_is_observation_only_never_enforced`, and `tests/test_observatory_flow.py::test_pipeline_session_unaffected_by_flow_mode_existing`. Cite Mytkowicz et al. for why this must be demonstrated rather than assumed, and phrase the claim as "non-interventional by construction, test-enforced" rather than "proven not to change results."

### What we may NOT claim

- **We do not open the weights black box at all.** Nothing here touches mechanistic interpretability. Amodei's problem is untouched by our work; we relocate the question, we do not resolve it. Say this in the abstract, not in a footnote.
- **n=1.** One session (`sess_20260728T031245`), 19 dashboard-traced turns plus 3 live night turns, one kernel (qwen3.5:0.8b, `think=false`), one host, one operator. The byte-share figures are a property of *this* substrate's packet design, not of deployed LLM products in general. A different application would yield different numbers. The claim that must generalize is the *structural* one (application-layer material dominates model input, and it is enumerable), not the specific percentages.
- **Byte share is not influence.** No causal attention claim. The repo already enforces this in its own language; the paper must not soften it.
- **The regime shift is confounded.** The morning/evening split coincides with commit `5a9eb6d` landing mid-session, so the two groups ran on different code paths. This is documented in the repo and must be disclosed, not smoothed over.
- **No controlled repeat trial exists.** The logs contain no clean same-input-twice A/B, so determinism claims are off the table; the byte-identical output collisions are evidence of an attractor, not of controlled reproduction.
- **Transparency ≠ accountability** (Ananny & Crawford), **and transparency can backfire** — explanations increase reliance regardless of correctness (Bansal et al.), and visible internals can amplify anthropomorphization in exactly the affective register our operator is in (Cox et al.). We can claim the window exists and is honest; we cannot claim it produces better calibration without a user study we have not run.
- **VizCopilot and RAGONITE exist.** Do not claim to be the first user-facing transparency layer for LLM applications. Claim the narrower, defensible thing: first *complete, byte-exact, per-turn* one aimed at a lay operator on consumer hardware.

## The gap we occupy

The scholarly and public black-box debate locates opacity in the model, while the transparency that has actually been built for the surrounding application layer is developer- and auditor-facing — traces, evals, prompt cards, lifecycle audit ledgers — or user-facing but partial, showing retrieved documents rather than the assembled input. Meanwhile the one line of work that asks end users what they want to see about the layer that steers their conversation (Neumann et al., CHI 2026) documents the demand and finds platforms actively concealing it, without building the artifact. We occupy the space between: a measurement of how small the human's contribution to model input actually is, and a working existence proof that the remaining 86–99.4% can be made completely and honestly inspectable, per turn, for a non-developer, on consumer hardware, without intervening in the conversation it observes.

## Suggested framing language for the paper

Each sentence is followed by the exact bound it carries.

**1.** "The black box is smaller than the experience: in the session reported here, the operator's own words were 0.56%–14.00% of the bytes entering the model across 19 traced turns, and the application layer supplied the rest — including, on at least one turn, the vocabulary in which the model then framed the exchange."
*Bound:* holds for one session, one kernel, one substrate design; the percentages are properties of this packet compiler, not of LLM products generally. The vocabulary claim is a single-turn qualitative observation with a published trace, not a frequency estimate.

**2.** "Opacity in a deployed conversational system is layered: the weights remain mechanistically inscrutable, but the assemblage around them — instructions, durable state, selected dialogue, output schema, constraints — is fully enumerable, and its opacity is therefore a design choice rather than a technical necessity."
*Bound:* this is a claim about *our* system's assemblage plus a general argument by construction; it does not assert that any commercial system could be made transparent at equal cost, and it says nothing about weights.

**3.** "We report a record, not an explanation: the Interior View reconstructs the exact payload bytes, per-check validation results, and a byte census of context composition, and we deliberately decline to call the byte census 'attention' — byte share is not influence."
*Bound:* the strongest sentence in the set, and the safest. It positions us outside the XAI faithfulness debate (Jacovi & Goldberg; Slack et al.; Ghassemi et al.) rather than inside it. Do not weaken it later by using attention-adjacent language in figures.

**4.** "The instrument is non-interventional by construction and by test: substrate observations are recorded and rendered but never block, never alter what was said, and state integration happens strictly after the exchange."
*Bound:* this is a code-level invariant with named test coverage, not an empirical A/B demonstration that observed and unobserved sessions produce identical outputs. Phrase as "by construction, test-enforced." Cite Mytkowicz et al. (2009) for why the distinction matters.

**5.** "Existing calls for transparency in LLM systems have been answered for developers and auditors; this work answers one item on that roadmap for the person in the conversation, on consumer hardware, with the raw session evidence published under a hash manifest."
*Bound:* we are not first to build user-facing transparency for LLM applications (VizCopilot, RAGONITE precede us); we claim completeness and audience, not priority. And publishing evidence is not the same as demonstrating that anyone can act on it — inspectability without a study of actionability or contestability (Guo & Wei's other two criteria) is the honest limit of what we have.

**Optional sixth, if the paper wants the rhetorical close:** "Pasquale's metaphor was always double — a black box is both the system nobody can see into and the recorder that survives the crash. This work builds the second kind, around the first."
*Bound:* rhetoric, and the duality is genuinely Pasquale's (confirmed), but it is a framing device and should not carry any technical claim.
