## Domain map

Domain C — small language models and edge deployment — has consolidated into five recognizable threads since 2024, and the conditioned-kernel work touches four of them.

**1. Sub-1B / small-model capability engineering.** The founding move was showing that architecture, not just scale, determines sub-billion quality (MobileLLM, ICML 2024), followed by data-centric proofs that 1-2B models can be pushed far past their weight class (Phi-3-mini, SmolLM2). Qwen2.5 and Qwen3 established the 0.5B / 0.6B / 0.8B tier as a *shipped product line* rather than a research curiosity — which is exactly the tier conditioned-kernel treats as replaceable. The field-defining measurement paper is Lu et al.'s survey of 70 open SLMs in the 100M-5B band, which benchmarks on-device latency and memory footprint directly rather than only accuracy.

**2. "Small models + strong scaffolding" as an argument.** This is the fastest-moving thread and the one most load-bearing for conditioned-kernel's thesis. Belcak et al. (NVIDIA, 2025) made the position argument: agentic invocations are narrow, repetitive, and format-bound, so SLMs are sufficient and economically necessary. Sharma & Mehta (2025) turned it into a survey with an explicit engineering claim: strict JSON Schema output plus validator-first execution "close much of the capability gap." Two 2026 papers close the loop empirically from the *harness* side rather than the model side: Ben Sghaier et al. held the LLM constant across 35 releases of a coding agent and traced quality shifts to the middleware, arguing practitioners systematically misattribute harness regressions to the model; Liu ran a full 2^5 factorial over five scaffold components and found maximally-equipped agents lose to smaller subsets (a 3-component subset beat "All-In" by 79% on GSM8K). The counterweight thread is long-horizon failure: SLM agents degrade sharply as horizon grows, and open-domain reasoning remains a documented fallback-to-large boundary.

**3. Structured output, constrained decoding, and the format-vs-quality tension.** Tam et al.'s "Let Me Speak Freely?" is the canonical warning that format restriction degrades reasoning; JSONSchemaBench (Geng et al., 2025) is the canonical rigorous measurement of whether constrained decoding actually delivers valid structure and at what cost. The unresolved question in this thread is precisely conditioned-kernel's F5 question: schema validity is not answer quality, and the two are measured by different instruments.

**4. Reasoning-mode / thinking-token behavior.** Qwen3 shipped hybrid think/no-think with a thinking budget; the follow-on literature is largely about how badly that control surface behaves. "Do NOT Think That Much" (ICML 2025) established overthinking as a first-class efficiency failure. "Demystifying Hybrid Thinking" (Wang et al., 2025) established *reasoning leakage* — no-think mode is an unstable halfway state, not a clean switch. "When Thinking Fails" (Li et al., 2025) found CoT *degrades* instruction-following in 13 of 14 models. And "Small Models Struggle to Learn from Strong Reasoners" established the learnability gap: models ≤3B do not reliably benefit from long-CoT distillation. Net: for the sub-1B tier, the thinking channel is a liability more often than an asset, and its control flag is not trustworthy.

**5. Edge systems: determinism, quantization behavior, hardware envelopes.** Xu et al.'s on-device review and Renney et al.'s 2026 single-board-computer benchmark define the deployment envelope. The behavioral-fidelity thread is newer and sharper: He / Thinking Machines Lab (Sept 2025) located temperature-0 nondeterminism in *batch-size non-invariance* of kernels rather than GPU float nondeterminism; Rababah et al. (July 2026) showed quantization produces behavioral divergence — measured by decision-level "correctness agreement" — that accuracy and perplexity do not detect. Both say the same structural thing: identical weights plus identical prompt does not imply identical behavior, and the standard metrics are blind to it.

**What this domain does not have much of:** per-turn, byte-level, publicly-receipted traces of what a sub-1B kernel actually did inside a running system on real edge hardware. The measurement culture is benchmark-aggregate (BFCL, IFEval, GSM8K, schema-validity rate). Nobody in this thread publishes 22 full inference traces with payload bytes and per-check validation outcomes.

## Key works

1. **The Curious Case of Neural Text Degeneration** — Holtzman, Buys, Du, Forbes, Choi. ICLR 2020. arXiv:1904.09751. Maximization-based decoding produces repetitive, degenerate loops; the seminal anchor for "the same text keeps coming out."
2. **MobileLLM: Optimizing Sub-billion Parameter Language Models for On-Device Use Cases** — Zechun Liu et al. ICML 2024. arXiv:2402.14905. Sub-1B is an architecture problem; 350M-class models reach "close correctness to LLaMA-v2 7B in API calling tasks."
3. **Phi-3 Technical Report: A Highly Capable Language Model Locally on Your Phone** — Abdin et al. Microsoft, 2024. arXiv:2404.14219. 3.8B at GPT-3.5-class MMLU; the reference point for "small enough to deploy on a phone."
4. **Small Language Models: Survey, Measurements, and Insights** — Zhenyan Lu, Xiang Li, Dongqi Cai, Rongjie Yi, Fangming Liu, Xiwen Zhang, Nicholas D. Lane, Mengwei Xu. arXiv:2409.15790 (Sept 2024; rev. Feb 2025). 70 open SLMs, 100M-5B, with on-device inference latency and memory footprint benchmarked.
5. **Qwen2.5 Technical Report** — Qwen Team. arXiv:2412.15115. Open sizes 0.5B/1.5B/3B/…/72B — the family the first-half kernel (`qwen2.5:0.5b`) comes from.
6. **Qwen3 Technical Report** — Qwen Team. arXiv:2505.09388. Dense + MoE, 0.6B-235B; unified thinking / non-thinking modes with a thinking budget.
7. **SmolLM2: When Smol Goes Big — Data-Centric Training of a Small Language Model** — Loubna Ben Allal et al. (22 authors). arXiv:2502.02737. 1.7B on ~11T tokens; the fully-open data-centric small-model reference.
8. **Small Language Models are the Future of Agentic AI** — Peter Belcak, Greg Heinrich et al., NVIDIA Research, 2025. arXiv:2506.02153. Position paper: SLMs are sufficient, more suitable, and more economical for most agentic invocations; includes an LLM-to-SLM conversion algorithm.
9. **Small Language Models for Agentic Systems: A Survey of Architectures, Capabilities, and Deployment Trade-offs** — Raghav Sharma, Manan Mehta. arXiv:2510.03847 (Oct 2025). "Strict JSON Schema outputs, and validator-first tool execution close much of the capability gap"; proposes *schema validity rate* as a first-class production KPI. Also names the boundary: open-domain reasoning and long-horizon planning still favor fallback to larger models.
10. **Don't Blame the Large Language Model: How Agent Harness Evolution Shapes Coding Agent Quality** — Oussama Ben Sghaier, Hao Li, Bram Adams, Ahmed E. Hassan. arXiv:2607.03691 (July 2026). Controlled longitudinal study holding the LLM constant across 35 releases of Qwen Code CLI on 50 stratified SWE-bench Verified tasks; harness-induced regressions are routinely misattributed to the model.
11. **More Is Not Always Better: Cross-Component Interference in LLM Agent Scaffolding** — Ming Liu. arXiv:2605.05716 (May 2026). Full 2^5 factorial over five scaffold components, Llama 8B and 70B: single-tool beats All-In by 32% on HotpotQA; a 3-component subset beats All-In by 79% on GSM8K; 56.3% submodularity violations.
12. **Let Me Speak Freely? A Study on the Impact of Format Restrictions on Performance of Large Language Models** — Zhi Rui Tam, Cheng-Kuang Wu, Yi-Lin Tsai, Chieh-Yen Lin, Hung-yi Lee, Yun-Nung Chen. arXiv:2408.02442 (Aug 2024). Format restriction significantly degrades reasoning; stricter constraints, worse degradation.
13. **JSONSchemaBench: A Rigorous Benchmark of Structured Outputs for Language Models** — Saibo Geng et al. (9 authors, incl. Michał Moskal, Robert West, Eric Horvitz, Harsha Nori). arXiv:2501.10868 (Jan 2025). 10K real-world JSON schemas across six constrained-decoding frameworks, scored on efficiency, coverage, and output quality.
14. **Demystifying Hybrid Thinking: Can LLMs Truly Switch Between Think and No-Think?** — Shouren Wang, Wang Yang, Xianxuan Long, Qifan Wang, Vipin Chaudhary, Xiaotian Han. arXiv:2510.12680 (Oct 2025). Mode separation is incomplete; reasoning leaks into no-think (≈1,085 → 585 tokens after their fix; reasoning-supportive phrases ≈5,917 → 522 on MATH500).
15. **When Thinking Fails: The Pitfalls of Reasoning for Instruction-Following in LLMs** — Xiaomin Li, Zhou Yu, Zhiwei Zhang, Xupeng Chen, Ziji Zhang, Yingying Zhuang, Narayanan Sadagopan, Anurag Beniwal. arXiv:2505.11423 (May 2025). Explicit CoT degrades instruction-following across 15 models and two benchmarks.
16. **Small Models Struggle to Learn from Strong Reasoners** — Yuetai Li, Xiang Yue et al. arXiv:2502.12143 (Feb 2025). The Small Model Learnability Gap: models ≤3B do worse with long-CoT distillation than with short, simple chains.
17. **Defeating Nondeterminism in LLM Inference** — Horace He and collaborators, Thinking Machines Lab, 10 Sept 2025. https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/defeating-nondeterminism-in-llm-inference/. Temperature-0 nondeterminism traced to batch-size non-invariance of RMSNorm/matmul/attention kernels; 1000 completions gave 80 unique outputs under default vLLM, 1 unique output with batch-invariant kernels.
18. **The Illusion of Equivalency: Statistical Characterization of Quantization Effects in LLMs** — Baha Rababah, Cuneyt Gurcan Akcora, Carson K. Leung. arXiv:2607.08734 (July 2026). Introduces decision-level "correctness agreement"; behavioral divergence emerges under moderate quantization while accuracy and perplexity look preserved; non-linear breakpoints at low bit-widths; Q/K projections more sensitive than V/O.
19. **Cloud to Edge: Benchmarking LLM Inference on Hardware-Accelerated Single-Board Computers** — Harri Renney, Fouad Trad, Michael Mattarock, Zena Wood. arXiv:2604.24785 (April 2026). Throughput-density and energy-per-million-tokens metrics across four IoT-suitable edge platform configurations.
20. **Task Competence Is Not Instruction Following: Evaluating Instruction-Conflicting Behavior in Small Language Models** — Mahdiyeh Farajidizaji, Vatsal Raina. arXiv:2607.19608 (July 2026). Instruction-tuned Qwen models across sizes; smaller models retain task competence while routinely disregarding conflicting instructions — capability and controllability are separate competencies.
21. **Mind the Confidence Gap: Overconfidence, Calibration, and Distractor Effects in Large Language Models** — Prateek Chhikara. arXiv:2502.11028 (Feb 2025; rev. Dec 2025). Nine models, three QA datasets; smaller models remain significantly miscalibrated even where prompting interventions help most.
22. **On-Device Language Models: A Comprehensive Review** — Jiajun Xu et al. arXiv:2409.00088 (Aug 2024). The deployment-envelope survey: quantization, pruning, distillation, hardware acceleration, edge-cloud collaboration.

## Where our findings sit

### F5 — validation vs quality; 93/93 schema-valid; "the architecture, not the model, blocked speech"

**(a) What the literature already says.** This is a *known and named* position, not a discovery. Sharma & Mehta (#9) argue explicitly that schema-constrained tasks are where SLMs are strongest and that schema validity rate should be a production KPI — which is to say, the field already expects a 0.5-0.8B kernel to hit near-100% JSON validity under a validator-first harness. Belcak et al. (#8) make the same bet at position-paper level. MobileLLM (#2) showed 350M-class models near-7B on API calling. Meanwhile Tam et al. (#12) predict that the *format constraint itself* costs reasoning quality, and JSONSchemaBench (#13) exists precisely because validity ≠ usefulness. So "high schema validity coexisting with poor answer quality" is the exact tension this subfield already frames. Ben Sghaier et al. (#10) is the strongest prior for our causal framing: hold the model constant, vary the harness, and quality moves — and practitioners misattribute it to the model. Liu (#11) adds that *more* scaffolding is frequently worse, with measured negative interference.

**(b) What our setting adds.** Three things the cited works do not have. First, the split instrument: we can report, on the same 93 candidates, that the kernel was 93/93 `parse_ok` with 0 `required_section` violations and 0/93 over the 120-word cap (max 73 words) — while the *substrate's* accept/repair/reject decisions rejected or repaired a large fraction of those structurally perfect outputs. That is a direct, per-item demonstration of the validity/quality gap that #9 and #13 argue about in aggregate. Second, an inadvertent within-day A/B on the kernel itself: the mid-session swap at commit `330128e` (qwen2.5:0.5b → qwen3.5:0.8b, `think=false`) moved median answer length 75 → 229 chars and acceptance 10/50 → 19/31, with 50 receipts confirmed pre-swap and 31 post-swap. That is a small-model-capability delta measured *through the same fixed harness*, which is the mirror image of #10's design (they held the model fixed and varied the harness). Third, the precision/recall decomposition of a single advisory check — 7/7 fires were real misses, but the check was blind to echo-without-answer, pasted-context token buffets, and second-person stance errors. #9 proposes schema-validity-rate as a KPI; we supply an instance where a *non-schema* lexical check was precision-1.0 and recall-poor, which is the failure mode a validity-rate KPI cannot see.

**(c) What we may NOT claim.** n=1 day, one session (`sess_20260728T031245`), two kernels from one family, one host. The swap was not preregistered as an A/B and confounds model change with the ~12-minute-later validator commit `b385157`; a 4x verbosity jump across a model swap is descriptive, not causal. We cannot claim our harness generalizes, cannot claim a *rate* of the validity/quality gap, and cannot claim the kernel would have answered well if the substrate had let it — we only observed that the gate, not the parser, blocked emission. And 93/93 validity is unremarkable on its own; the literature predicts it. The contribution is the paired trace, not the number.

### Thinking-channel probes (Qwen think-flag wire behavior)

**(a) What the literature already says.** Fully anticipated, from three directions. Qwen3 (#6) documents hybrid think/no-think as a shipped control surface. Wang et al. (#14) show that surface is leaky and that no-think is "an unstable halfway mode." "Do NOT Think That Much" (ICML 2025, #16-adjacent, arXiv:2412.21187) established overthinking as excessive compute for minimal benefit. Li et al. (#16) established that ≤3B models are the *worst* beneficiaries of long CoT. Li et al. (#15) showed CoT actively degrades instruction-following. So "a sub-1B Qwen burns thousands of tokens in the reasoning channel and produces a worse or absent final answer" is a predicted outcome, not a novel one.

**(b) What our setting adds.** A wire-level, harness-level instance with exact bytes, on real edge hardware. Per `/Users/vaquez/conditioned-kernel/experiments/THINKING_MODE_FINDING.md` (Jetson Orin Nano 8GB, Ollama 0.24.0, CUDA, MAXN_SUPER, plain `/api/generate`, one neutral prompt, no packet/schema/`format=`): `qwen3.5:0.8b` returned in **121.0 s** with **4,090 eval tokens**, **16,214 chars in the `thinking` field, and 0 chars in `response`**, against `qwen2.5:0.5b` at **2.1 s / 117 tokens / no `thinking` field / 644 chars**. The failure was *doubly* fatal: 121 s exceeded the 90 s edge-profile timeout, and even on return the harness's `extract_text` reads only `message.content` or `response` and never `thinking`. The genuinely new observation is the **methodological one**: this model's ladder score of `0.000` was recorded as a quality measurement and was actually an *empty read* — a benchmark number produced by an instrumentation gap, not by the model. The literature on thinking-mode leakage (#14) does not cover the case where the evaluation harness structurally cannot see the reasoning channel and silently scores it as failure. That is a concrete, receipted instance of benchmark-number invalidity in the SLM edge setting, and the repo names it as the fifth such catch that day. Under the eventual deployment config (`think=false`), `telemetry.thinking_chars == 0` for all 22 traced passes and a regex scan of all 93 `raw_text` bodies for `<think>`/`</think>` returned zero matches — so in the measured session the channel was genuinely closed, and `generate.py`'s hard-coded `NO_FINAL_RESPONSE` path for this incident never fired.

**(c) What we may NOT claim.** This is one prompt, one probe, one runtime version. We did not read the `thinking` field's contents, so we have **no quality claim whatsoever** about qwen3.5:0.8b in thinking mode — the repo says this explicitly and the paper must repeat it. We cannot claim think=false is "safe" in general (#14's leakage result argues the opposite in general); we can only say that in 22 traced passes under this config the channel emitted zero characters. Also note a scope caveat the paper must honor: `experiments/MODEL_QUALIFICATION.md` was run on **Apple Silicon, not the Jetson**, and marks `qwen3.5:*` as UNVERIFIED-on-Jetson pending re-qualification. **One number to reconcile before publication:** the task brief cites "1 s with think=false, no answer in 240 s without." What I can verify in the repo is 121.0 s (not 240 s) for the thinking case and 1.309 s (`MODEL_QUALIFICATION.md`, qwen2.5:0.5b) / 2.1 s (`THINKING_MODE_FINDING.md`) for the fast cases. If a 240 s Jetson re-run exists it is not in `experiments/THINKING_MODE_FINDING.md`, `MODEL_QUALIFICATION.md`, or `docs/observations/`; do not print 240 s without a receipt.

### Edge-first design constraints (8 GB Jetson-class, one model, short context) — and F-D1..F-D4 determinism

**(a) What the literature already says.** Xu et al. (#22) and Renney et al. (#19) define the envelope; Lu et al. (#4) benchmark latency/memory across 70 SLMs. On determinism, He / Thinking Machines Lab (#17) is the definitive statement of the *mechanism class* — identical inputs at temperature 0 give different outputs because kernels are not batch-invariant. Rababah et al. (#18) is the definitive statement that quantization changes behavior in ways accuracy and perplexity do not detect, with non-linear breakpoints at low bit-width. So "quantized edge inference is behaviorally unstable in ways standard metrics miss" is established.

**(b) What our setting adds.** `/Users/vaquez/conditioned-kernel/experiments/DETERMINISM.md` reports an axis the cited work does not cover: **VRAM load state**. On a Jetson Orin Nano 8GB with `qwen2.5:0.5b` Q4_K_M (digest `a8b0c515…`), Ollama 0.24.0, locked clocks, single-request (so batch size is *not* the variable #17 identifies), the same prompt produced two perfectly stable attractors keyed to cold-vs-warm: COLD_A/COLD_B/COLD_C all `2a8061e5c31c`, WARM_A/WARM_B both `8cef27259503`. Three controls make it clean: the same model digest and byte size on Apple Silicon/Metal (Ollama 0.20.7) gave one hash across all seven cold and warm runs, matching the Jetson's *cold* answer (F-D2); moving to `qwen2.5:0.5b-instruct-fp16` eliminated the split entirely across six runs (F-D3); and the substrate state hash was `ab670e93` before and after every sequence, excluding persistent state as a variable. The repo then scopes itself honestly: F-D4 reports **1 of 4 prompts affected**, and refuses the headline "Q4 on CUDA is nondeterministic" in favor of "some generations sit near a token decision boundary where Q4_K_M rounding plus a cold/warm CUDA kernel-path change is enough to flip the outcome." This is a complementary mechanism to #17 (kernel-path selection by residency, not reduction order by batch size) and a concrete instance of #18's thesis (a behavior difference that no accuracy metric would surface) on an 8 GB edge board. It also matters for F2: a byte-identical 285-char answer emitted 7 times at temp 0.3 / seed 42 is *not* automatically evidence of an attractor in the model, because on this exact hardware the same weights can emit two different stable answers depending on residency — the paper should cite its own F-D1 as the reason F2's byte-identity is a substrate-plus-runtime fact rather than a pure model fact.

**(c) What we may NOT claim.** One board, one runtime version, one quantization pair, four prompts, one model. We did not vary batch size, so we cannot connect our effect to #17's mechanism; we did not read kernel dispatch, so "CUDA kernel path" is inference from the fp16 control, not direct observation. We cannot claim a prevalence rate for the cold/warm flip beyond 1/4 prompts on this prompt set. We cannot claim fp16 is generally safe — only that at F16 this boundary was not reached for these prompts. And we should not present the Jetson determinism work as covering `qwen3.5:0.8b`; it does not.

### F2 (attractor genealogy) and F3 (gate blindness / confidently-wrong goal) — Domain C's contribution

**(a) What the literature already says.** Repetition and degenerate loops under maximization-flavored decoding are the Holtzman et al. (#1) result, and temp 0.3 is well inside the regime where the reliable tail dominates. Small-model overconfidence is documented: Chhikara (#21) finds smaller models remain significantly miscalibrated; Farajidizaji & Raina (#20) find small Qwen models retain task competence while ignoring conflicting instructions — capability and controllability come apart. So a 0.5B kernel asserting a false project goal with high surface confidence, three times, is squarely a *predicted* small-model behavior, and a stale-repeat check that only consults the last accepted turn is a scaffold defect of exactly the kind Liu (#11) and Ben Sghaier et al. (#10) locate in the harness rather than the model.

**(b) What our setting adds.** Distance-resolved gate telemetry: 4/23 re-emissions caught at distance 1 and 0/12 at distance ≥2, with the 285-char byte-identical answer traced across unrelated prompts and across a full session reset. The literature predicts the phenomenon; we can show the *lookback window* that produced it, per turn, with hashes. Likewise F3's asymmetry — checks that detect echoing the true goal but not asserting a false one — is a concrete, receipted instance of #20's competence/controllability split expressed as a validator design flaw.

**(c) What we may NOT claim.** Nothing causal about why the attractor formed; no claim about model interiority; no claim that the persistence "survived reset" implies memory in any sense other than that durable project state was recompiled into the packet. Single session, single kernel family.

### The substrate framing itself (F1, F6, F9)

**(a) What the literature already says.** Ben Sghaier et al. (#10) is the closest prior and should be cited as such, plainly: "the middleware scaffolding layer — not the language model itself — significantly determines agent effectiveness," established by a controlled longitudinal study. Liu (#11) supplies the negative-interference half. Belcak et al. (#8) and Sharma & Mehta (#9) supply the economic and validator-first halves. Our thesis sentence — "the model supplies linguistic possibility; the substrate determines what becomes an answer" — is a sharper *statement* of a claim this domain has already made empirically. The paper should say so in the related-work section rather than framing it as new.

**(b) What our setting adds.** The byte census. #10 varies harness releases and measures task success; nobody in this thread measures *how much of the model's input the human actually wrote*. 0.56%-14% of model-input bytes, median ~2%, with the substrate's own vocabulary resurfacing in the kernel's output, is a quantity this domain does not currently report — and it is available only because the harness archives exact payload bytes per turn. Combined with F6's admission-vs-authorship split (selected material fully unused in 11/26 cases), it gives the scaffolding-dominance thesis a measurement it currently lacks.

**(c) What we may NOT claim.** Byte share is explicitly **not** attention, influence, or causal contribution, and the paper already labels it so — keep that label prominent, because a reader will otherwise read 2% as an attribution claim. Descriptive, n=1, one host, one kernel family, one context-compilation strategy. And nothing about interiority; F7's own framing ("everything around the experience-question, nothing claimed inside it") is the right posture and should not drift.

## The gap we occupy

This domain measures small models in aggregate — schema-validity rates, BFCL and IFEval scores, tokens/sec and joules on a board — and it measures harnesses by outcome deltas across releases. What it does not have is a *per-inference forensic record*: the exact bytes sent to a sub-1B kernel on an 8 GB edge board, the per-check validation outcome, the accept/repair/reject decision, and the resulting emission, all hash-manifested and public. Conditioned-kernel provides that record for 93 candidates and 22 full traces, plus two controlled edge probes the literature's mechanism papers do not cover — a load-state (cold/warm) determinism split under Q4_K_M that fp16 removes, and a documented case where a benchmark score of 0.000 was an instrumentation-blind empty read of a thinking channel rather than a model failure. The contribution is the instrument and the receipts discipline, not the discovery of any of these phenomena: substrate dominance, thinking-channel liability in sub-1B models, quantization-induced behavioral divergence, and scaffold-not-model attribution are all already established here, and the paper is stronger for saying that first.
