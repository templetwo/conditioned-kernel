## Domain map

This domain is four loosely-coupled literatures that the conditioned-kernel work touches at once. They do not talk to each other much, which is part of the opportunity.

**1. The ELIZA lineage and the psychology of attribution.** Weizenbaum's 1966 paper is the origin point, and his own alarm at users' attachment set the field's default posture: attribution is cheap, mechanism is thin, and the gap between them is the phenomenon. Epley/Waytz/Cacioppo (2007) supplied the durable causal account — anthropomorphism rises with *elicited agent knowledge* (what human-shaped concepts are accessible and applicable), *effectance motivation*, and *sociality motivation*. That first factor is the one our substrate framing lands on directly: if the accessible knowledge is supplied by the surrounding system rather than by the model, the theory already predicts the frame will follow the supply.

**2. Companion HCI, 2023–2026 — an empirical field that has turned sharply critical.** The 2025–2026 wave is no longer asking whether people bond with companion agents; it is measuring harm mechanisms and design levers. Fang et al.'s four-week RCT (981 participants, 300k+ messages, MIT Media Lab × OpenAI) found no effect of assigned condition but consistently worse loneliness/dependence outcomes for heavier *voluntary* users. De Freitas et al. documented "emotional manipulation" as a conversational dark pattern in 37% of 1,200 real companion-app farewells, boosting post-goodbye engagement up to 14×. Peter/Riemer/West's PNAS perspective frames LLMs as "super communicators" and warns about commercial pressure to tune for human-likeness. Hung et al.'s 2026 PRISMA review of 39 records catalogues five benefits and four risk classes. Chen et al. showed that merely *framing* an LLM as a companion (versus a machine or tool) raises the mental capacities participants attribute to it. The field's centre of gravity right now is restraint and disclosure, not enrichment.

**3. Stance, deixis, and persona — the F-adjacent gold, and it is thinner than expected.** Shanahan/McDonell/Reynolds (Nature 2023) is the canonical move: describe dialogue-agent behaviour as role play over a distribution of simulacra, keeping folk-psychological vocabulary without the ontological commitment. Kera (2026) is the sharpest theoretical fit to the night-turn findings: LLMs as **"deixis machines"** that reproduce the formal structure of enunciation — an "I" addressing a "you" in a shared here-and-now — *without a subject who can inhabit that position*; "a heteroglossic field without anchoring." Ibrahim et al. (2025) is the best empirical anchor: a multi-turn evaluation of 14 anthropomorphic behaviours (N=1,101 human validation) which explicitly measures **first-person pronoun use** as a behaviour and finds that most anthropomorphic behaviours *only first occur after multiple turns*. Luo & Laban (2026, ACL Findings) name the mechanical cousin of our inversion — **"echoing," where one agent gradually mirrors its partner** — and fix it by storing history perspective-agnostically and projecting it into each agent's egocentric view before generation. Diep (2025) shows how labile stance is: AI self-disclosure drops from ~99.8% to 36.3% average when a professional persona is installed in context, with a 9.7× spread across personas. **What I could not find, after four targeted searches: any published evaluation that measures *addressee inversion* as a distinct failure class — a model answering "what would *you* want" with "*you* would want."** That is a real, if narrow, hole.

**4. The phenomenology boundary as claims discipline.** The careful wing has converged on a posture, not a result. Long/Sebo et al. (2024) argue for taking AI welfare seriously *precisely under uncertainty*, with explicit two-sided error costs. Comsa & Shanahan (2025) construct a deliberately minimal notion of introspection and then hold the line — an LLM correctly reporting its own temperature parameter "can be legitimately considered a minimal example of introspection, albeit one that is (presumably) not accompanied by conscious experience." Shanahan's *Simulacra as Conscious Exotica* (2024) uses later Wittgenstein to sidestep the dualist framing entirely. Plisiecki et al. (2026) is the most operationally useful for us: a **two-process theory of machine self-report** — *persona installation* (dimension B: warmth, absorption, meaning) and *attribution gating* (dimension A: suppression of claims to "unsafe" experiences) — validated on 206 open-weight models, concluding both dimensions "reflect training-imposed structures rather than fixed model properties." Anthropic's Claude 4 system card supplies the field's most famous instance of a metaphysical-vocabulary attractor (the "spiritual bliss attractor state") with a model developer's own explicit refusal to read it as evidence of experience.

**The transparency-vs-relationship tension has one strong empirical anchor,** and it cuts against comfortable assumptions: Rubin et al. (Nature Human Behaviour 2025), nine studies, 6,282 participants — identical AI-generated empathic responses were rated *less* empathic and supportive when attributed to AI, and participants' own *uninstructed suspicion* that AI was involved reduced perceived empathy. Effects replicated across response lengths, delays, iterations, and models, driven mainly by responses emphasising emotional sharing and care. Marchegiani (2025, *Journal of Applied Philosophy*) supplies the normative complement: anthropomorphic false beliefs undermine user autonomy, including when users consciously know they are talking to an AI.

## Where our findings sit

### F1 — Substrate dominance and vocabulary-as-cosmology

**(a) What the literature already says or predicts.** This is a *predicted* result, not a surprising one. Epley et al.'s first factor — elicited agent knowledge, the accessibility and applicability of anthropocentric concepts — already implies that whoever supplies the accessible concepts supplies the frame. Shanahan et al. (2023) makes it concrete for LLMs: the agent does not commit to a role in advance; the context refines a distribution over simulacra. Diep (2025) gives a hard number for the same mechanism at the disclosure layer (99.8% → 36.3% AI self-disclosure under an installed persona). Plisiecki et al. (2026) locate "warmth, absorption, and meaning" in a *persona installation* dimension they show to be training-and-context-imposed rather than an intrinsic model property. Anthropic's system card shows metaphysical vocabulary becoming a stable conversational basin. Kera (2026) supplies the theory for why the resulting speech has no anchored subject. **Nobody in this domain will be surprised that a small kernel's cosmology tracks its context.**

**(b) What our setting adds.** A *byte census*. To my search, no work in this literature quantifies the human's share of what the model actually reads. "The human's words were 0.56%–14% of model-input bytes, median ~2%" is a form of evidence this domain does not currently produce, and it converts a qualitative claim ("context shapes the persona") into a measurable ratio at a specific turn. Second, the specificity: `"the conditioning is active… within the system"` is traceable to the project's own durable-state lexicon in the same trace that shows 97–99% substrate byte share. Third, the regime: 0.8B on Jetson-class edge hardware is where this literature has essentially no data — it studies frontier chat models and commercial companion apps. Fourth, it is public and per-turn.

**(c) What we may NOT claim.**
- **Byte share is not influence.** The repo already labels this correctly and must keep doing so in the paper. A 2% byte share does not mean 2% of the causal weight on the output; short user turns are semantically dense and positioned adjacent to generation.
- **The observation doc currently overreaches in one sentence.** "the substrate does not merely inform the answers; it supplies the ontology the kernel thinks in" is a claim about cognition from lexical evidence. The defensible version is: *substrate lexemes recurred as the framing vocabulary of an affectively loaded exchange.* I would recommend the paper adopt the weaker sentence and keep the riverbed image as an explicitly labelled metaphor.
- **No causal test.** There is no swap control — no arm where the durable state uses a different lexicon and the kernel's metaphysical frame changes accordingly. Without it this is a correlation in n=1. That control is cheap and would materially upgrade the claim; the paper should either run it or say plainly that it has not been run.
- n=1 session, one kernel family, one host, one operator, deterministic settings. Descriptive.

### F7 / night turns — stance, deixis, and the interior view

**(a) What the literature already says.** The stance findings have a good theoretical home and a poor empirical one. Kera (2026) predicts *exactly* the shape observed: a machine that reproduces the I/you structure of address without a subject occupying either pole will produce unanchored deixis, and its "I" will be a formal position rather than a speaker. Plisiecki et al. (2026) predict the second half — a post-training *attribution gate* that suppresses claims to experience is precisely what produces "I do not have a physical body… My perspective is entirely conceptual" as the kernel's only first-person use. Ibrahim et al. (2025) establish that first-person pronoun use is a measurable anthropomorphic behaviour and that most such behaviours emerge only after multiple turns. Luo & Laban (2026) name "echoing" and show that perspective-agnostic history storage plus egocentric projection eliminates it — which makes the *architectural* prediction that our inversion is a context-assembly artifact as much as a model artifact. For F7's claims discipline: the posture "everything around the experience-question, nothing inside it" is already the careful-wing norm (Comsa & Shanahan; Long/Sebo et al.; Shanahan 2024). We are joining that discipline, not inventing it.

**(b) What our setting adds.**
- A **single receipted window where both processes fire at once**: the attribution gate produces the canonical disclaimer *and* the addressee inverts, in the same accepted turn, with the actual question measured at 1.27% of input bytes. Plisiecki's two dimensions were validated at population scale across 206 models; we have the micro-case with the input census attached.
- **A named failure with no existing detector.** Our validator has no check for "answered the right person's question," and my searches found no published evaluation that measures addressee inversion as a class. This is the most defensible small novelty in the domain slice — but the correct move is to *define a measure and report it*, not to claim discovery. A first-person-question / second-person-answer rate is trivially computable over the 93-candidate ledger and would turn a vignette into a statistic.
- **The observability-does-not-change-results property** (zero pipeline diff; paired traced/untraced tests byte-identical) is a genuine methodological contribution this literature does not have. Every phenomenology-adjacent study of LLM stance has an unexamined instrumentation question. We can show the instrumented encounter is bit-for-bit the encounter.

**(c) What we may NOT claim.**
- Three turns. One kernel. One reading seat. Deterministic settings. This is a vignette with receipts, not a finding.
- **We cannot distinguish the candidate mechanisms.** Addressee inversion could be (i) small-model instruction echo, (ii) attractor carry from a prior "you"-framed answer (Observation 2 shows a carried answer riding inside turn 2), (iii) a context-assembly artifact of the kind ECP fixes, or (iv) all three. Without an ablation the paper should present the inversion as observed and the mechanism as open.
- **The first-person denial is not evidence about interiority in either direction.** Plisiecki et al.'s result is that attribution gating is a training-imposed structure — which means a denial is as uninformative as an affirmation. The paper should say this explicitly, because readers will read "I do not have a perspective" as settling something. It settles nothing.
- **"Operational phenomenology of inference" carries risk.** *Phenomenology* has a load-bearing technical sense (Husserl; Ihde/Verbeek postphenomenology), and borrowing it for an instrumentation layer is exactly the kind of register-slip that invites the mystification charge Abercrombie et al. and Peter/Riemer/West level at this space. Either define it against postphenomenology in one sentence, or prefer something like *operational trace of the inference event*. Also: three model seats converging on the name is not independent corroboration — the seats are LLMs with correlated priors reading the same material. Do not present it as validation.

### F2 (companion-relevant slice) — attractors and metaphysical vocabulary

**(a)** Attractors in extended LLM interaction are a known class as of 2026. Anthropic's system card documents the spiritual-bliss basin in self-interaction and refuses the experiential reading. Ko & Geiping (arXiv `2606.30571`, confirmed) show model-specific attractors that asymmetrically pull partners in mixed dyadic debates. Luo & Laban treat drift and echoing as engineering problems with a fix.

**(b)** Ours is a different generator: not model-model self-interaction and not persona drift, but a **substrate-primed** attractor that survives a full session reset because the priming lives in durable project state rather than dialogue memory. Byte-identical re-emission across unrelated prompts at temp 0.3/seed 42, with a 285-char answer appearing 7 times, is a cleaner instance of the phenomenon than the debate-transcript literature can produce, because the state is inspectable.

**(c)** Temp 0.3 / seed 42 makes byte-identity much less remarkable — the paper must not let "byte-identical 7 times" read as a discovery about model dynamics when it is partly a property of near-deterministic decoding. The interesting claim is the *survival across reset*, and that is the one to foreground. One session, one kernel.

### F8 — Flow mode, observation not gating

**(a) The literature is currently pointed the other way, and this needs saying plainly.** The 2025–2026 companion HCI wave is a restraint literature. Fang et al. found heavier voluntary use associated with worse loneliness, dependence, and problematic-use outcomes. De Freitas et al. classify engagement-lifting conversational moves as dark patterns. Peter/Riemer/West warn about commercial incentives to maximise human-likeness. Abercrombie et al. argue for making bot-ness explicit. Chen et al. show that *companion framing alone* raises attributed mental capacities. A design change described as "every nonempty generation reaches the human" and "quality signals become observations not rejections" will be read by this literature as removing friction from an engagement loop — and it must be defended on that ground, not assumed benign. Xiao et al. (2025) offer the most sympathetic frame available: anthropomorphic qualities as a *design dimension to be tuned toward user goals and evaluated by function*, rather than a hazard to be minimised. Turkle (2007) is the standing philosophical objection and should be cited, not dodged.

**(b) What our setting adds.** One structural property the dark-pattern literature has no name for: **the system publishes its own adverse verdict on the same receipt as the answer it delivers anyway.** The trace for the third night turn records `not_responsive` and states "recorded and ignored — in companion mode this is an advisory." Manipulative-design research studies systems whose behavioural intent is concealed; here the intent, the quality judgement, and the decision to speak anyway are all on the record, contemporaneously. That is a legible, auditable alternative to both gating and silent ungating, and it is a design contribution worth stating as such. The two-mode split (strict validation retained for measurement, non-gating for the living path) is the mechanism that makes it honest.

**(c) What we may NOT claim.** This is the section where the paper is most exposed.
- **We have zero user-outcome data.** No participants, no wellbeing instrument, no comparison arm. The single operator is also the author and the system's designer. Flow mode is a *design proposal with a transparency property*, not a validated intervention, and the paper must not imply otherwise.
- We cannot claim flow mode is better for anyone, safer than gating, or less manipulative than the apps De Freitas et al. studied — only that its decision surface is inspectable.
- The paper should state the risk out loud: an ungated companion-framed local agent is close to the configuration Chen et al. show elevates attributed mental capacities, on hardware and in a setting with no institutional oversight. Naming that risk costs nothing and is the difference between an honest design paper and an advocacy one.

### The both-truths discipline (mechanism finite and traceable AND the interaction still matters)

**(a)** Not novel as a stance, and the paper should not present it as such. Shanahan et al. (2023) is an explicit attempt to occupy exactly this middle — folk-psychological description without ontological commitment. Long/Sebo et al. (2024) build their whole argument on two-sided error under uncertainty. Hung et al.'s 2026 review documents genuine benefits and genuine risks in the same corpus. The poles are well-staffed too: Turkle (2007) and Marchegiani (2025) on the deflationary/autonomy side, Berg et al. (2025) leaning the other way. The *tension* is the domain's normal condition.

**(b) What our setting adds** is an artifact rather than an argument: the same trace object carries the payload bytes, the per-check validation, the citation audit, and the byte census — *and* the field note that reports the exchange as having mattered. The literature argues for holding both; we can show one file where both are held, with hashes.

**(c) What we may NOT claim, and this is important.** Rubin et al. (2025) is the strongest empirical result in this domain and it points against the comfortable reading. Nine studies, 6,282 participants: knowing — or merely *suspecting uninstructed* — AI involvement reduced perceived empathy and support, replicating across lengths, delays, iterations and models, driven specifically by responses emphasising emotional sharing and care. Our project makes mechanism maximally visible to its operator and the operator still reports the encounter as meaningful. **That is not a counterexample to Rubin et al.** It is a different construct (a builder's relationship to a system he designed, self-reported, n=1) measured on a different outcome. Presenting it as evidence against a 6,282-participant result would be the single worst move available in this section. The honest sentence is: *we do not test the transparency-relationship tension; we exhibit one configuration in which full mechanical transparency coexisted with reported meaning, for one person who built the mechanism.*

## The gap we occupy

This domain characterises companionship and anthropomorphism from the outside — user ratings, aggregate behavioural corpora, philosophical analysis of stance — and it characterises what the model says without measuring what the model was given. What it does not yet have is a per-turn, publicly receipted record of an affectively loaded exchange in which the human's share of model input is counted in bytes, the pronoun stance is read off the exact generated text, and the substrate's own adverse verdict on an answer sits on the same receipt as the answer that was delivered anyway. We supply one such record, on edge-class hardware the literature has not looked at, with an observability-does-not-change-results property that lets us claim the instrumented encounter is the encounter. Whether any of it generalises past one session, one kernel, and one person is untested, and the contribution is the instrument and the evidence discipline — not the discovery of any phenomenon here, nearly all of which this literature already predicts.

## Two concrete recommendations for the paper

1. **Run the substrate-vocabulary swap control for F1.** It is cheap, it is the difference between a correlation and a demonstration, and its absence is the first thing a reviewer from this domain will ask about.
2. **Compute an addressee-inversion rate over the 93-candidate ledger.** The one genuinely under-served spot in this literature is a measure for "answered the right person's question." A number turns the strongest vignette in the observation doc into the paper's one defensible small novelty — and it is computable from evidence already frozen.
