## Domain map

The subfield relevant to F7 is not one literature but four that have only recently started touching each other:

**1. Transparency documentation (2018–2024).** Model cards [1], datasheets [2], and system cards [3] established a genre: a static, human-readable artifact accompanying a released model or dataset that discloses provenance, intended use, evaluation, and limitations. The Foundation Model Transparency Index [4] turned the genre into a scored, comparative instrument (100 indicators, upstream/model/downstream) and documented that transparency has been *declining* even as impact grows. **Structural property of this whole lineage: the unit of disclosure is the model or the organization, and the temporal grain is the release.** Nothing in it discloses a single inference event.

**2. LLM/agent observability engineering (2024–2026).** This is where the per-call unit lives. AgentOps [6] gave the first academic taxonomy of what artifacts should be traced across an agent lifecycle. The OpenTelemetry GenAI semantic conventions [7] are now the de-facto standard schema — spans, metrics, and events for GenAI clients, with attributes like `gen_ai.request.model`, `gen_ai.usage.input_tokens`, `gen_ai.response.finish_reasons`. A 2026 survey [8] consolidates the field around *execution provenance as a typed execution graph* and *evidence tracing as its projection onto support relations*, covering seven threads including observability and failure diagnosis. A companion 2026 result [9] proves a sharp negative: audit logs and execution traces can be **identical under multiple incompatible delegation assignments** — traces underdetermine what actually happened unless context is bound at execution time. CHI EA 2025 [10] studied 30 developers and named four design pillars (Awareness, Monitoring, Intervention, Operability), finding that intervention-optimized tools *reduce* mental-model clarity while awareness-first designs deepen it but slow response.

**3. Provenance / reproducibility infrastructure.** Atlas [11] is the current shape of the art: fully attestable ML pipelines, cryptographic hashes of precursor artifact attestations embedded in each transformation attestation, signed, forming a verifiable lineage chain. Adjacent surveys report that only ~17% of ML lifecycle tools offer real provenance functionality. This literature is about *supply chain integrity of models*, not about the evidentiary integrity of a behavioral study's raw traces.

**4. Glass-box HCI and the interpretability boundary.** Liao & Vaughan [5] is the roadmap paper: transparency is fundamentally about supporting appropriate *human* understanding, stakeholder-relative, and they argue documentation-only approaches are insufficient for LLMs. AI Chains [S7] established the canonical HCI move — decompose the pipeline into inspectable steps to buy transparency and controllability. Seamful XAI [S3] argues for *revealing* sociotechnical seams rather than hiding them. Everyday Algorithm Auditing [S4] establishes that non-expert users in situ surface harms that formal audits miss. On the other side of the boundary: mechanistic interpretability [14] (34M sparse-autoencoder features on Claude 3 Sonnet), and — critically for us — its recent migration into user-facing dashboards: TalkTuner [S1] surfaces a probed internal "user model" to lay chat users, and Karny et al. [S2] surface live neural activations across turns in an n=246 RCT and show improved user calibration to behavioral drift. **The weights-level literature is now colonizing exactly the UI surface F7 occupies — but with different content.**

Cutting across all four: the epistemics of "what counts as showing influence." Jain & Wallace [12] is the durable anchor — attention weights are frequently uncorrelated with gradient-based importance, and different attention distributions yield equivalent predictions, so distributional share is not explanation. ContextCite [13] is the constructive answer: to claim a generated statement was caused by a piece of context, you ablate context and measure, you do not count tokens. Turpin et al. [S6] close the loop from the other side: model self-reports of their own reasoning systematically misrepresent the actual cause, which is precisely why external instrumentation is the honest route.

## Where our findings sit

### F7 — Interior View, the observability-does-not-change-results property, and byte-census honesty

**(a) What the literature already says or predicts.** Nearly every *component* of the Interior View has prior art, and the paper should say so in the first paragraph rather than the limitations section.

- *Capturing exact payload bytes, model params, and per-call metadata.* Fully standardized [7], taxonomized [6], and commercially ubiquitous. Not novel.
- *Hash-manifested, tamper-evident artifact chains.* Atlas [11] does this with signed attestations and transparency logs. Our SHA-256 manifest is a simpler instance of an established pattern.
- *Trace-as-evidence with typed provenance relations and citation/support projection.* Named and taxonomized in [8]. Our citation audit is an instance of "evidence attribution" in their taxonomy.
- *Per-turn dashboards beside a chat UI, for lay users.* TalkTuner [S1] and Karny et al. [S2] already exist and already ran user studies.
- *Instrumentation should not perturb the measured system.* Standard systems requirement; and [S5] shows on Jetson-class hardware that this cannot be assumed — sub-3% overhead suffices to alter measured behavior, and measurement infrastructure is itself an interference-sensitive resource.
- *Share-of-input is not influence.* [12] settled this in 2019; [13] shows what a real influence claim requires.
- *Awareness-first observability design deepens mental models.* Predicted by [10].
- *Documentation-genre transparency is insufficient for LLMs; human-centered, use-time transparency is needed.* Argued in [5]. The Interior View is essentially an instance of what that roadmap called for, three years later, on a small model.

**(b) What our setting adds.** Four things, none of which is "we invented tracing."

1. **The unit is the inference event boundary, not the span tree.** Production observability decomposes a *request* into spans and reports cost/latency/tokens/eval scores per span. The Interior View instead reconstructs *the totality of one stateless invocation*: every byte the kernel received, apportioned by contributing source; the exact generation parameters; every validation check with its individual verdict; the citation audit; and the acceptance decision. To my search, no published artifact reconstructs the complete input-side composition of a single call and presents it to a non-developer operator as the primary object. The closest neighbors surface *internals* (TalkTuner's probed user model [S1], Karny's activations [S2]) or support *authoring* (AI Chains [S7], PromptAid [S8]) — they do not answer "what exactly was this model handed, and by whom, and what was checked before you saw the reply."
2. **Content-first and public, against the field's default.** The OTel default is explicit: no prompt content captured, because it can contain sensitive data [7]. Production LLM observability is therefore *structurally* metadata-first and content-redacted. Our artifact inverts that default and publishes the content corpus. This is affordable precisely because of the regime — one operator, one edge host, a 0.5–0.8B kernel, no sampling required — and it is what makes the artifact a research instrument rather than an ops dashboard. **This is also the sharpest practical claim in the paper: F3 (gate blindness — 0/12 stale re-emissions caught at distance ≥2; a false project goal accepted 3×) is invisible under default-configured production observability, because catching it requires retained answer *content* across turns, not span metadata.**
3. **A named, deliberately non-causal measure.** The byte census is presented as *share of input bytes by source*, explicitly not attention, not attribution, not influence. There is no OTel attribute for "share of input contributed by source X," and no convention for labeling such a measure's epistemic status. Given [12] and [13], the discipline of naming the measure and then naming what it is *not* is the contribution here — not the arithmetic.
4. **The neutrality property was tested, not assumed.** Zero pipeline diff; paired traced/untraced runs byte-identical at fixed temperature/seed. [S5] is the reason this matters and the reason it deserves a line in the paper: on the same class of hardware, software-reported timing masked failures that external observation caught. Publishing the paired-run check is cheap and almost nobody does it.

**(c) What we may not claim.**

- **Not prior-art-free, in any component.** Payload capture, hash manifests, provenance graphs, trace UIs, and per-turn dashboards all exist and are cited above. The honest claim is *composition and posture*, not invention.
- **Byte census ≠ attention ≠ causal influence.** F1's "0.56%–14% human share, median ~2%" is a fact about *what was supplied*. It licenses no statement about what drove the output. To claim the substrate *caused* the answers we would need ContextCite-style ablation [13], which we did not run. The vocabulary-surfacing observation ("the conditioning is active... within the system") is suggestive and should be framed as a hypothesis generator, not evidence.
- **"Operational phenomenology of inference" is a naming, not a finding, and not phenomenology.** A search for the term in AI/ML returned no prior art; the nearest neighbors are interiority-claiming works like [S10]. That absence is not a moat — the practice it names (structured reconstruction of the observable event) is what [6], [7], [8] already do. The term earns its place only if the paper states plainly that it denotes *everything around* the experience question and asserts nothing inside it. Three model seats converging on the phrase is a provenance note, not evidence.
- **Zero-diff is one configuration, not a theorem.** One host, one kernel family, one parameter set. [S5] demonstrates that observability neutrality is architecture-dependent and interference-sensitive. State the config and the test, not a general property.
- **No efficacy claim for the dashboard.** We ran no user study. [S2] (n=246 RCT) and [10] (30 developers) are the bar for "this improves understanding/calibration." We may say the instrument made F3 and F6 *findable by us*; we may not say it makes anything findable by users in general. n=1 operator.
- **Traces underdetermine.** [9] is directly on point and should be cited against ourselves: a complete-looking trace can be consistent with multiple incompatible accounts of what happened. Our reconstruction is *of the recorded event boundary*, which is a strictly weaker object than "what the system did."
- **Not a standards contribution.** We have not mapped the trace schema to OTel GenAI semantic conventions [7]. Until we do, this is a bespoke instrument, and saying so costs nothing.

### F5 — validation vs quality (precision-strong 7/7, recall-poor; 93/93 schema-valid)

*Already predicted.* [10] and [S6] both name the gap: trace logging is not evaluation depth; EDDOps [S6] exists precisely because static benchmarks and schema checks do not capture open-ended behavior, and argues for evaluation as continuous runtime governance rather than a one-time gate. Our contribution is a **quantified per-check receipt** for the gap on a real corpus — a lexical advisory that fires 7/7 correctly and is blind to echo-without-answer, pasted-context token buffets, and second-person stance errors, in a system that is simultaneously 93/93 JSON-schema-valid. The sentence "the architecture, not the model, blocked speech" is well supported by our data and is a clean, citable instance of a class the literature asserts abstractly. **Bound:** one advisory implementation, one taxonomy of misses that we ourselves defined post hoc; the recall denominator is our own labeling, so report it as descriptive, not as a validated metric.

### F8 — flow mode (observations, not rejections)

*Partially anticipated.* [S6]'s offline-vs-runtime evaluation split maps almost one-to-one onto our measurement-mode/living-path split, and Seamful XAI [S3] gives the design argument — hiding the seams disempowers the user; reveal them and let the human mitigate. [10]'s finding that intervention-optimized tools reduce mental-model clarity is a *direct* empirical prediction of the flow-mode thesis and should be cited as such. **What we add:** a concrete architecture where the quality signal is retained but demoted from gate to annotation, with the strict path preserved for measurement, and traces showing both regimes on the same kernel. **What we may not claim:** any user-facing benefit. We have no study showing the human is better served. Frame as a design position with a working implementation, and cite [10]/[S3] as the prior arguments it operationalizes.

### F6 / F3 — what the instrument made visible

The live A/B (3 rejected before, 16 accepted after context-field switchover) and the selection-governs-admission-not-authorship result (selected material fully unused in 11/26; the attractor's carrier text selected-in twice, answer Jaccard 1.00) are exactly the kind of finding that [7]'s content-off default forecloses. This is the strongest *instrumental* argument in the paper: **the finding is a function of the retention policy.** But note the honest bound — "selected but unused" is itself a byte-level/lexical judgment, not a causal one; [13] would be needed to say material was *not used* in any influence sense. Prefer "did not surface in the output" over "was unused."

### F9 — method and evidence discipline

*Established practice, unusual application.* Hash-chained artifact attestation is the state of the art in ML supply-chain provenance [11]; documentation-as-accountability is a seven-year lineage [1][2][3]; the FMTI [4] shows the field knows how to score disclosure and knows disclosure is falling. **What our setting adds is the object being frozen**: not model weights or training data, but the *raw behavioral trace corpus of a study*, with the analysis scripts, published whole. Retiring an audited manifest because its control arm was shown to contain the gold answer is preregistration hygiene imported from other fields — not novel as a norm, notable as a *published* act with receipts. **Bound:** we have no signed attestations, no transparency log, no external verifier — Atlas-grade integrity is a strictly stronger property than a SHA-256 manifest in a git repo. Say what we have (tamper-evident against accidental drift, auditable by anyone who clones) and what we don't (cryptographic non-repudiation).

## The gap we occupy

Production LLM observability standardized the *span* and made content capture opt-out-by-default for privacy reasons; transparency documentation standardized the *release*; mechanistic interpretability standardized the *feature* — and the two 2026 user-facing dashboards that come closest to our artifact both surface model internals rather than the model's input. Nothing in this domain currently offers a complete, content-retaining, publicly published reconstruction of a *single inference event boundary* — every byte supplied and by whom, every check and its verdict, the acceptance decision — built for a lay operator on edge hardware, with the share-of-input measure explicitly disclaimed as non-causal and the instrumentation's own neutrality empirically tested rather than assumed. We do not claim a new phenomenon or a new mechanism; we claim an instrument with an unusual retention posture, an honest label on its weakest measure, and a public corpus that lets someone else check both.
